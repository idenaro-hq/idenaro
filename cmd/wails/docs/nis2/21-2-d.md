Article 21(2)(d) addresses security in supply chains, including security-related aspects concerning the relationships between each entity and its direct suppliers or service providers. It requires that entities assess and manage security risks from third-party relationships.

A finding mapped to this article indicates that an identity provisioning or integration interface is exposed in a way that represents risk not just to your systems but to any partner or customer connected to your identity infrastructure.

### What it means in practice

SCIM provisioning endpoints are the primary supply chain vector in IAM systems - they allow external identity providers and HR systems to create, update, and deprovision user accounts. An unauthenticated or over-permissioned SCIM endpoint gives any connected supplier the ability to manipulate your identity store. Chain findings also map here when the combination of weaknesses spans the boundary between your system and external integrations.

### Compliance obligation

- SCIM provisioning endpoints must require authentication and be restricted to your provisioning system's network range.
- Third-party access to identity management systems must be individually audited, scoped, and minimised.
- Supplier security practices must be assessed as part of onboarding and reviewed periodically.
- Contracts with identity providers and HR system vendors must include security requirements aligned with Art. 21.

### Enforcement context

Supply chain security has become a priority enforcement area post-SolarWinds and similar incidents. Supervisory authorities are specifically looking at how entities manage third-party access to critical systems, making exposed provisioning interfaces a high-visibility compliance gap.
