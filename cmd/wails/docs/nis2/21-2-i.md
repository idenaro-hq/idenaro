Article 21(2)(i) requires human resources security, access control policies, and asset management as core cybersecurity measures. This covers who has access to what, under what conditions, and how that access is governed throughout the asset and identity lifecycle.

A finding mapped to this article means the scanner detected a weakness in how your system governs access to identity and authentication infrastructure.

### What it means in practice

This is the broadest IAM-specific category: insecure OAuth2 flows (implicit, ROPC, device flow misuse), SAML assertion signing failures, OIDC algorithm weaknesses, missing IAM controls, user enumeration vulnerabilities, open redirect attacks on auth flows, exposed SCIM provisioning endpoints, exposed admin panels, zero-trust gaps, token leakage, and lifecycle management failures including dormant accounts and missing offboarding.

### Compliance obligation

- Access control policies must define roles, permissions, and review cycles for all identity infrastructure.
- Authentication protocols must use current, secure flows - Authorization Code + PKCE for OIDC, signed assertions for SAML.
- Identity provisioning (SCIM) must be protected by authentication and restricted to authorised systems.
- Access tokens and credentials must never be transmitted through browser-facing channels (URL fragments, localStorage).
- Password reset and authentication flows must not reveal account existence information.
- Asset inventory must include all identity systems, and access to them must be reviewed at defined intervals.

### Enforcement context

Art. 21(2)(i) is the most frequently cited article in IAM-related enforcement actions. Authentication infrastructure is explicitly considered critical - supervisory authorities view missing access controls on administrative systems as a high-severity non-compliance.
