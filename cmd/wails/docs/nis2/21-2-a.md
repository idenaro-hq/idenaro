Article 21(2)(a) requires entities to have risk analysis and information system security policies as a foundational cybersecurity measure. This means systematically identifying, assessing, and documenting risks to network and information systems.

A finding mapped to this article indicates a configuration that either introduces unquantified risk or reflects the absence of a deliberate security policy decision - for example, an exposed administrative surface or an unrestricted cross-origin policy.

### What it means in practice

Findings in this category include publicly reachable admin interfaces (no policy restricting administrative access), permissive CORS configurations (no policy defining trusted origins), and zero-trust gaps (absence of a deny-by-default access model). Chain findings also trigger this article because a combination of weaknesses signals the absence of a cohesive security policy.

### Compliance obligation

- A formal risk register must cover network-facing administrative interfaces and identity infrastructure.
- Security policies must explicitly define which origins, networks, and principals may access each system.
- Zero-trust architecture decisions must be documented - if full zero-trust is not yet implemented, a remediation roadmap is required.
- Risk assessments must be reviewed at defined intervals and after significant changes.

### Enforcement context

Supervisory authorities treat the absence of documented risk analysis as a process-level failure independent of technical outcomes. Organisations that cannot demonstrate a risk assessment process face enforcement action even if no breach has occurred.
