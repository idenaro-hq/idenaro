Article 21(2)(h) requires entities to implement policies and procedures regarding the use of cryptography and, where appropriate, encryption. This covers all aspects of how cryptographic mechanisms are selected, configured, and maintained across systems.

A finding mapped to this article means the scanner detected a weakness in the cryptographic controls protecting data in transit or cryptographic material used for authentication.

### What it means in practice

Findings in this category include broken or outdated TLS configuration (expired certificates, deprecated protocol versions such as TLS 1.0/1.1, weak cipher suites), session cookies transmitted without the Secure flag (allowing plaintext interception), and token security weaknesses involving weak signing algorithms or insufficient entropy. These represent direct failures of the cryptographic policy Art. 21(2)(h) requires.

### Compliance obligation

- All authentication traffic must be encrypted using current TLS standards (minimum TLS 1.2, prefer TLS 1.3).
- HSTS must be deployed with a minimum max-age of 31,536,000 seconds to prevent SSL stripping.
- Session tokens must be protected via the Secure cookie flag and HTTPS-only configuration.
- Token signing must use approved algorithms (RS256, ES256 minimum) with keys of appropriate length.
- A cryptographic policy document must define approved algorithms, key lengths, and rotation schedules.

### Enforcement context

Under NIS2, national supervisory authorities can impose fines up to €10 million or 2% of global annual turnover for essential entities, and €7 million or 1.4% for important entities, for non-compliance with Art. 21 measures.
